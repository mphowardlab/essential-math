7.1 Substitution

Some second-order ODEs can be solved by making a substitution

u=d⁡ ⁣yd⁡ ⁣xu = \dd{}{y}{x}u=dxdy​
(1)

Then, you effectively “integrate twice”: once to solve for u, then once more to get y from u.

Example: Kinematics¶

Newton’s second law is F=maF = maF=ma, where F is the applied force, m is the mass, and a is the acceleration of a body. We also know that acceleration is the derivative of the velocity v, which is itself the first derivative of position x. Hence, Newton’s second-law is a second-order ODE:

md⁡ ⁣2xd⁡ ⁣t2=Fm \dd{2}{x}{t} = Fmdt2d2x​=F
(2)

Make the substitution v=d⁡ ⁣x/d⁡ ⁣tv = \d{x}/\d{t}v=dx/dt, then integrate because the ODE is separable

d⁡ ⁣vd⁡ ⁣t=−g∫d⁡ ⁣v=∫−gd⁡ ⁣tv=−gt+c1\begin{align} \dd{}{v}{t} &= -g \\ \int \d{v} &= \int -g \d{t} \\ v &= -g t + c_1 \end{align}dtdv​∫dvv​=−g=∫−gdt=−gt+c1​​​
(4)

Replace v and integrate again:
