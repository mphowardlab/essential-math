5 First-order ordinary differential equations

Motivation¶

Differential equations relate the derivatives of an unknown function to the function, the function’s derivatives, and other variables. They arise frequently in physics and engineering. For example, Newton’s second law, F=ma\vv{F} = m \vv{a}F=ma, is a differential equation because the acceleration a=x′′\vv{a} = \vv{x}''a=x′′ is the second derivative of the position x\vv{x}x with respect to time ttt. The force F\vv{F}F may be a function of ttt, x\vv{x}x, and velocity v=x′\vv{v} = \vv{x}'v=x′, depending on the physics:

mx′′=F(t,x,v)m \vv{x}'' = \vv{F}(t, \vv{x}, \vv{v})mx′′=F(t,x,v)
(1)

In chemical engineering, differential equations are often used to model the rate of change of quantities with respect to time or how they vary in space. Differential equations are needed to:

  • Model unsteady processes, such as batch reactors, where process variables are expected to change over time.

  • Model the startup of a process as it comes to steady state, as well as how it behaves if it departs from steady state. The latter case is especially important for control theory!

  • Model how transport processes cause mass or energy to distribute in space. For example, differential equations are needed to model transport phenomena, such as diffusion and heat transfer, as well as to design certain types of process equipment, such as plug flow reactors.

Example: Batch reactor

A batch reactor is a closed reaction vessel: no mass enters or exits after the reaction is started and before it is stopped. Hence, the concentrations of the reactants and products in a batch reactor continuously change as the reaction progresses.

The liquid volume in the reactor is VVV, and it can be assumed to be constant density regardless of composition. The total number of moles of A, nAn_{\rm A}nA​, is related to the concentration and the volume VVV by nA=cAVn_{\rm A} = c_{\rm A} VnA​=cA​V. Write the unsteady mole balance for A:

(2)

Then substitute this relationship, the given rate of reaction, and that there are no flows in or out of a batch reactor:

We will learn how to solve for cA(t)c_{\rm A}(t)cA​(t) in this chapter!

Learning goals¶

  1. Define a first-order ordinary differential equation and explain its significance in chemical engineering.

  2. Select between analytical and numerical methods for solving first-order ordinary differential equations.

  3. Solve unsteady mass and mole balances such as those involving tanks and reactors and unsteady energy balances such as those found in heat-transfer applications.

Preqrequisite knowledge¶

You should make sure you are comfortable with the following:

  • Integration techniques, including substitution, by parts, and partial fraction decomposition.

  • Evaluating limits.
