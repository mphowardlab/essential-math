3.2 Systems of linear equations

A system of linear equations has the form:

a11x1+⋯+a1nxn=b1⋮am1x1+⋯+amnxn=bn\begin{align} a_{11}x_{1} + \cdots + a_{1n}x_{n} &= b_{1} \\ \vdots \\ a_{m1}x_{1} + \cdots + a_{mn}x_{n} &= b_{n} \end{align}a11​x1​+⋯+a1n​xn​⋮am1​x1​+⋯+amn​xn​​=b1​=bn​​​
(1)

There are n unknown variables, (x1,⋯ ,xn)(x_1, \cdots, x_n)(x1​,⋯,xn​) and m equations. The m equations have coefficients amna_{mn}amn​ for each of the n variables and m right-hand side values (b1,⋯ ,bm)(b_1, \cdots, b_m)(b1​,⋯,bm​). These equations be rewritten using matrices and vectors:

[a11⋯a1n⋮⋮am1⋯amn][ x1⋮xn]=[b1⋮bm]\begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & \cdots & a_{1n} \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & \cdots & a_{mn} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \ x_{1} \\ \vdots \\ x_{n} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b_{1} \\ \vdots \\ b_{m} \end{bmatrix}⎣⎡​a11​⋮am1​​⋯⋯​a1n​⋮amn​​⎦⎤​⎣⎡​ x1​⋮xn​​⎦⎤​=⎣⎡​b1​⋮bm​​⎦⎤​
(2)

or in shorthand:

Ax=b\vv{A}\vv{x} = \vv{b}Ax=b
(3)

A system of linear equations can have:

  • No solution (inconsistent equations)

  • One solution

  • Infinitely many solutions (undetermined)

For example, consider the following graphs of different systems of 2 equations:

  • x+y=1x+y = 1x+y=1, x+y=0x+y = 0x+y=0

    This system of equations has no solution because the two lines are parallel and never intersect.

  • x+y=1x + y = 1x+y=1, 2x−y=02x - y = 02x−y=0

    This system of equations has one solution because the two lines intersect at a point.

  • x+y=1x+y = 1x+y=1, 2x+2y=22x + 2y = 22x+2y=2

    This system of equations has infinitely many solutions because the two lines are parallel and completely overlap.

Types of systems

For a system of linear equations Ax=b\vv{A}\vv{x} = \vv{b}Ax=b, if the matrix A is m x n, then we say that the system is:

  • Undetermined if m<nm < nm<n (fewer equations than unknowns). The system has no unique solution.

  • Determined if m=nm = nm=n (same number of equations and unknowns).

  • Overdetermined: m>nm > nm>n (more equations than unknowns)
