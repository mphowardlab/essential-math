3.4 Determinants

For a square nxn matrix A, the determinant is denoted in a few different ways:

det⁡A=∣A∣=∣A11⋯A1n⋮⋮An1⋯Ann∣\det \vv{A} =|\vv{A}|=\begin{vmatrix} A_{11} & \cdots & A_{1n} \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ A_{n1} & \cdots & A_{nn} \end{vmatrix}detA=∣A∣=∣∣​A11​⋮An1​​⋯⋯​A1n​⋮Ann​​∣∣​
(1)

The determinant is defined in a recursive way.For n=1n=1n=1, ∣A∣=A11|\vv{A}|= A_{11}∣A∣=A11​ ( the matrix element) For n≥2n \ge 2n≥2, ∣A∣|\vv{A}|∣A∣ is defined as:

∣A∣=∑j=1nAijCij=∑j=1n(−1)i+jAijMij|\vv{A}|= \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij} C_{ij} = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{i+j} A_{ij} M_{ij}∣A∣=j=1∑n​Aij​Cij​=j=1∑n​(−1)i+jAij​Mij​
(2)

where i is any row of A, CijC_{ij}Cij​ is the cofactor of A:

Cij=(−1)i+jMij,C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} M_{ij},Cij​=(−1)i+jMij​,
(3)

and MijM_{ij}Mij​ is the minor of A. The minor is the determinant of the matrix obtained by removing row i and column j from A. Equivalently,

∣A∣=∑i=1nAijCij=∑i=1n(−1)i+jAijMij|\vv{A}| = \sum_{i=1}^n A_{ij} C_{ij} = \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+j} A_{ij} M_{ij}∣A∣=i=1∑n​Aij​Cij​=i=1∑n​(−1)i+jAij​Mij​
(4)

where now j is any column of A.

2x2 matrix¶

2x2 determinant

The determinant of a 2x2 matrix is:

∣abcd∣=ad−bc\begin{vmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{vmatrix} = a d - bc∣∣​ac​bd​∣∣​=ad−bc
(5)

We will use the definition of the determinant to show this must be the case! Let’s use the first definition and pick the first row i=1i=1i=1:

∣abcd∣=(−1)1+1⋅A11M11+(−1)1+2A12M12=a∣abcd∣−b∣abcd∣=ad−bc\begin{align} \begin{vmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{vmatrix} &= (-1)^{1+1} \cdot A_{11} M_{11} + (-1)^{1+2} A_{12} M_{12} \\ &= a \begin{vmatrix} \phantom{a} & \phantom{b} \\ \phantom{c} & d \end{vmatrix} - b \begin{vmatrix} \phantom{a} & \phantom{b} \\ c & \phantom{d} \end{vmatrix} \\ &= a d - b c \end{align}∣∣​ac​bd​∣∣​​=(−1)1+1⋅A11​M11​+(−1)1+2A12​M12​=a∣∣​ac​bd​∣∣​−b∣∣​ac​bd​∣∣​=ad−bc​​
(6)

Larger matrices¶

The determinants of larger matrices can be computed by reducing them to sums of 2x2 determinants. To do this quickly, it can be helpful to envision (−1)i+j(-1)^{i+j}(−1)i+j as a checkerboard of signs, then visualize the minors.

For example, to evaluate

∣130264−102∣\begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 2 & 6 & 4 \\ -1 & 0 & 2 \end{vmatrix}∣∣​12−1​360​042​∣∣​
(7)

The sign matrix is:

[+−+−+−+−+]\begin{bmatrix}+ & - & + \\ - & + & - \\ + & - & + \end{bmatrix}⎣⎡​+−+​−+−​+−+​⎦⎤​
(8)

Even faster, start from the plus sign in the upper left corner, then alternate until you get to your chosen row or column! Let’s use row 3:

∣A∣=+(−1)⋅∣3064∣−0⋅∣1024∣+2⋅∣1326∣=−(3⋅4−0⋅6)+2(1⋅6−2⋅3)=−12\begin{align} |\vv{A}| &= + (-1) \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 6 & 4 \end{vmatrix} - 0 \cdot \begin{vmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 \end{vmatrix} + 2 \cdot \begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 6 \end{vmatrix} \\ &= -(3 \cdot 4 - 0 \cdot 6) + 2(1 \cdot 6 - 2 \cdot 3) \\ &= -12 \end{align}∣A∣​=+(−1)⋅∣∣​36​04​∣∣​−0⋅∣∣​12​04​∣∣​+2⋅∣∣​12​36​∣∣​=−(3⋅4−0⋅6)+2(1⋅6−2⋅3)=−12​​
